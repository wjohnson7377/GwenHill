Who is Gwen?

Gwen — Gwendolyn Paulhill — was my mother. G-W-E-N-D-O-L-Y-N

If you've ever wondered where the name on this place comes from, now you know: it's hers. The GwenHill Resource Center is named in her honor — Gwen from her name, and Hill from Paulhill — so her name is the first thing you meet when you come here looking for help. Who she was and why this place exists go hand in hand, and they belong together, right here.

My mother was a single parent who worked hard every single day of her life. I honestly can't remember a moment when I didn't see her working hard — that was just who she was. She carried our family on her own, and she did it without ever letting up.

Everything I know about being a good person, I learned from her. She taught me manners and values. She taught me what it means to respect yourself, to try hard, to work hard, and to never give up. But most importantly, she taught me to be true to my family — and to be a good person, through and through. She raised me, and I could not have asked for a better mother. I could not have been blessed with a better parent.

I want people to know she went through real struggles, because I saw them with my own eyes. I saw her fall, and I saw her pick herself back up and go right back to working hard — and she did that with no help at all. She never asked for anything. And even at her lowest, she never let herself stay down. She never let herself fall for good.

That's why I say this plainly: my values come from her. My ethic comes from her. My work ethic comes from her. My everything comes from her. She is the birth of all of this.

“This hub was built out of my trials and tribulations.”

This place was built out of my own trials and tribulations. Going through them taught me, firsthand, what it takes to move forward — and what kind of help you actually need when you're trying to. I came to know what was needed because I needed it myself: one place, no runaround, just real help you could actually find when you went looking for it. And I knew if I needed it, there were others out there needing it just as much. So that's what I'm trying to give them. Nobody should have to get back up with no help at all, the way my mother so often did — not if there's a way to stand beside them.

So I built the thing I wished I'd had, and I put my mother's name on it. That's what the GwenHill Resource Center is: her strength, turned outward. The training, the certifications, the food, housing, internet, and benefits gathered here — all of it is free help and free resources for people who need them, given the way she lived: with hard work, with dignity, and without asking anything in return. I want this place to really help people, and I want it to stay completely free. I know that's hard for people to believe these days. Everything seems to come with a catch, a fee, and another catch behind the fee. Not here.

Sometimes you just help because you want to help. That's reason enough.

That's who Gwen is, and that's how GwenHill came to be — her name and her example, standing together at the door to welcome you in, and to make sure you don't have to face a hard season without help the way she so often did.

W
William Founder, GwenHill Resource Center — named for my mom, Gwendolyn Paulhill
